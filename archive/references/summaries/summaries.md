# Reference summaries

One summary per reference. Each opens with why the work matters for ARCHON, then summarises it and lists what ARCHON takes from it: mechanisms to model, patterns to validate against, and caveats.

**Start with:** Joseph and Gaba (the field today), Sah and Stiglitz (approval structures), Radner (processing limits), Prendergast (distorted reporting), Levitt 2012 (the closest precedent) and Grimm et al. (how to document the model).

**Tiers.** *Recent syntheses* state what the field knows now. *Foundations* are the original sources of results ARCHON reproduces as test targets: Sah and Stiglitz, Radner, Christensen and Knudsen, the three Csaszar papers, Prendergast, and Alonso, Dessein and Matouschek. *Background* keeps works for context only.

## Recent syntheses

| Reference | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| [Joseph and Gaba (2020)](2020_Joseph_Gaba_Organizational_Structure_Information_Processing_and_Decision-Making_AMA_summary.md) | The entry point: how formal structure shapes decisions through information processing, what has held up since the classics, and open gaps a simulator can test. |
| [Garicano and Rossi-Hansberg (2015)](2015_Garicano_Rossi-Hansberg_Knowledge-Based_Hierarchies_Annual_Review_CEP_OP043_summary.md) | Knowledge hierarchies: routine problems solved at the front line, exceptions escalated to experts; exact span, layer and pay results as test targets. |
| [Puranam, Stieglitz, Osman and Pillutla (2015)](2015_Puranam_Stieglitz_Osman_Pillutla_Modelling_Bounded_Rationality_in_Organizations_AMA_summary.md) | Which rules for simulated people are behaviourally grounded: a four-element agent interface and evidence on learning, exploration and search. |

## Decision structure and information processing

| Reference | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| [Sah and Stiglitz (1986)](1986_Sah_Stiglitz_The_Architecture_of_Economic_Systems_Hierarchies_and_Polyarchies_summary.md) | Founding model of approval structures: sequential chains and any-one-approves arrangements trade one error type for the other; closed forms for validation. |
| [Radner (1993)](1993_Radner_The_Organization_of_Decentralized_Information_Processing_summary.md) | Limited processing capacity: exact delay-versus-headcount results, queue growth under recurring load, processing network distinct from authority. |
| [Christensen and Knudsen (2010)](2010_Christensen_Knudsen_Design_of_Decision-Making_Organizations_summary.md) | One accept/reject graph for any approval structure, with analytic bounds, majority optimality and bias correction as checks. |
| [Csaszar (2012)](2012_Csaszar_Organizational_Structure_Determinant_of_Performance_Mutual_Funds_WP_summary.md) | Empirical test on 609 mutual funds: consensus cuts commission errors, independent approval cuts omissions; total error unchanged. |
| [Csaszar (2013)](2013_Csaszar_An_Efficient_Frontier_in_Organization_Design_WP_summary.md) | Exact error rates for every small structure and an efficient frontier: few structures are efficient, which is the case for redesign search. |
| [Csaszar and Eggers (2013)](2013_Csaszar_Eggers_Organizational_Decision_Making_Information_Aggregation_View_WP_summary.md) | When delegation, voting or averaging works best, given the spread of expertise, routing errors and environmental change. |
| [Clément and Puranam (2018)](2018_Clement_Puranam_Searching_for_Structure_Formal_Organization_Design_Network_Evolution_MS_SSRN2974436_summary.md) | How a lightly enforced formal chart, even a random one, steers the informal network; model the two as separate, co-evolving layers. |

## Knowledge, incentives and authority

| Reference | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| [Hayek (1945)](1945_Hayek_The_Use_of_Knowledge_in_Society_summary.md) | ARCHON's premise: knowledge is dispersed and local; prices coordinate through compressed signals, a benchmark for hierarchy. |
| [Prendergast (1993)](1993_Prendergast_A_Theory_of_Yes_Men_summary.md) | Subjective evaluation makes subordinates slant reports towards the superior's view, degrading information and centralising decisions. |
| [Alonso, Dessein and Matouschek (2008)](2008_Alonso_Dessein_Matouschek_When_Does_Coordination_Require_Centralization_summary.md) | Where authority should sit: greater need for coordination worsens vertical and improves horizontal communication, with exact thresholds. |
| [Gavetti et al. (2012)](2012_Gavetti_Greve_Levinthal_Ocasio_Behavioral_Theory_of_the_Firm_Assessment_and_Prospects_summary.md) | Behavioural decision rules for simulated agents: satisficing, adaptive aspirations, problem-driven search, routines, coalitions and attention. |
| [Cohen and Levinthal (1990)](1990_Cohen_Levinthal_Absorptive_Capacity_A_New_Perspective_on_Learning_and_Innovation_summary.md) | How organisations take in outside knowledge: it depends on related prior knowledge and interface structure, giving cumulative learning and lockout. |

| Reference | Why it matters |

## Empirical patterns at society scale

| Reference | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| [Acemoglu et al. (2019)](2019_Acemoglu_Naidu_Restrepo_Robinson_Democracy_Does_Cause_Growth_summary.md) | Benchmark: inclusive, accountable governance raises output per head gradually, by about 20% after 25 years. |
| [Colagrossi, Rossignoli and Maggioni (2020)](2020_Colagrossi_Rossignoli_Maggioni_Does_Democracy_Cause_Growth_Meta-Analysis_summary.md) | Tolerance for governance checks: the effect on growth is small, positive on average and strongly context-dependent. |
| [Oparina, Clark and Layard (2025)](2025_Oparina_Layard_Clark_Easterlin_Paradox_at_50_summary.md) | Grounds separate output and well-being objectives: in rich countries, growth shows no significant well-being gain. |
