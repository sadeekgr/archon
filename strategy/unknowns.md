## Supply-chain ordering game as a first test organisation

A chain of four roles, each seeing only its own incoming orders and shipping with a delay, reliably turns small changes in end demand into large swings upstream (the bullwhip effect). Sharing end demand along the chain dampens it. Human ordering behaviour in this setting has been measured experimentally and fits a simple rule that neglects goods already in transit.

- Why it matters: it isolates ARCHON's premise (beliefs differing from reality, delays, local decisions) with shared goals and no incentive conflict, and comes with human data to compare against.
- Open: whether to adopt it as a first test organisation, alongside two-person approval structures.
- Needs, if adopted: the published experimental data and fitted rule parameters.

## Queueing results as exact checks

- Little's law: work in progress equals throughput times time in system, for any stable queue. A free invariant for every in-tray and backlog.
- Delay grows sharply as utilisation approaches full load, and faster when work arrives irregularly. Hand-checkable, and the reason overloaded roles become bottlenecks.
- Pooling reserves or specialists absorbs variation better than keeping them separate.
- Open: which of these to adopt as validation checks, and at which step of the action plan.
